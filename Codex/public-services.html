Hospitals — future-proof care by design

Hospital facilities are designed for a 60-year life cycle and are required to support both day-to-day care and major emergency surges. Standards focus on patient safety, infection resilience, staff wellbeing, continuity of service and adaptability as healthcare models evolve.

  • Emergency and elective capacity — planned patient flow, emergency surge capacity, separation of clean and dirty routes, and fast access to diagnostics.
  • Resilient services — standby power, water continuity, digital connectivity, climate adaptation and safe transfer arrangements during outages.
  • Healthy staff and patient environments — natural light, ventilation, low-noise design, infection control, mobility access and comfortable recovery spaces.
  • Flexible clinical units — adaptable wards, modular treatment rooms and spaces that can be repurposed for future specialisms or population growth.

Fire and rescue stations — future-proof emergency response by design

Fire stations must support operational readiness, crew welfare and rapid mobilisation in all conditions. The standard requires robust, safe and efficient facilities that can serve growing populations while remaining usable during prolonged emergencies or severe weather events.

  • Operational capacity — appliance bays, crew rooms, decontamination areas, equipment storage and secure access for rapid turnout times.
  • Life safety and resilience — fire-safe construction, emergency lighting, flood resilience, back-up power and continuity of communications.
  • Workforce support — welfare areas, sleeping facilities, fitness spaces, quiet rest areas and modern incident command rooms.
  • Adaptability for future risk — electric appliance readiness, battery charging, drone and digital command support, and flexible response spaces for major incidents.

Fire station capacity

  • Station crew40–80 people
  • Appliance bays2–6 bays
  • Regional support footprintUp to 200 people
  • Response resilience24/7 ready

Police stations — future-proof public safety by design

Police facilities need to combine public access, operational security, staff safety and flexible civil-protection functions. Future-proofing means designing buildings that can respond to changing policing models, digital evidence needs and community-facing services.

  • Public service and secure operations — safe public entrances, reception areas, interview rooms, custody support zones and clearly separated operational functions.
  • Digital evidence and command — secure data environments, analysis rooms, dispatch coordination, CCTV monitoring and resilient communications systems.
  • Staff and community safety — safe circulation, trauma-informed spaces, accessible public facilities and secure movement between functions.
  • Flexible site use — room layouts that can adapt for officer safety training, community engagement, rural policing or major incident command.

Police station capacity

  • Local station150–250 people
  • Regional hub250–350 people
  • Public-facing operationsHigh access
  • Operational resilienceMulti-use

Prisons — future-proof secure facilities by design

Prisons must be secure, dignified, operationally efficient and resilient to changing demand. The design standard prioritises safety, mental and physical wellbeing, secure movement, staff capacity and the ability to adapt to different security categories over time.

  • Secure population management — safe cell clusters, controlled movement, staff visibility, segregation and de-escalation spaces that support safer operations.
  • Human dignity and rehabilitation — natural light, day rooms, education and healthcare spaces, and designs that reduce harm and improve behavioural outcomes.
  • Operational resilience — secure communications, emergency response routes, energy independence, water resilience and flood-safe site planning.
  • Flexible capacity planning — adaptable housing blocks, health suites, programme rooms and assessment units able to respond to population changes.

Education facilities — future-proof learning by design

Schools, colleges and universities are designed as long-life civic assets that support learning, wellbeing, access and public value over decades. These buildings must be resilient, healthy, digital-ready and adaptable to changing teaching methods, student numbers and community use.

  • Schools — safe, welcoming, accessible learning environments with flexible classrooms, daylight-rich teaching spaces, resilient heating and cooling, and secure arrival and exit arrangements.
  • Colleges — flexible teaching blocks, practical learning workshops, higher technical training spaces, student wellbeing hubs and strong links to local employment and civic life.
  • Universities — multi-use campuses with laboratories, research spaces, student accommodation, digital infrastructure, accessible circulation and resilient energy systems for long operating hours.

School, college and university standards

  • Schools — classrooms sized for modern teaching, inclusive access, acoustic control, accessible toilets, secure entrances, outdoor learning and healthy indoor air quality.
  • Colleges — vocational and academic teaching zones, specialist labs, digital learning spaces, student support centres and resilient digital infrastructure for blended learning.
  • Universities — research and teaching labs, lecture halls, libraries, student amenities, accommodation and campus-wide energy, water and digital resilience.
  • Shared design principles — daylight, accessibility, low-carbon heating, safe movement, outdoor space, future-ready digital networks and capacity for community use beyond core hours.

Future-proofing priorities

  • SchoolsWarm, safe, inclusive
  • CollegesFlexible & employable
  • UniversitiesResearch-ready & resilient
  • All campusesLow-carbon & accessible

Common future-proofing standards across all categories

  • Design for a minimum 60-year life cycle with modular adaptation and straightforward maintenance.
  • Plan for climate resilience, energy continuity, water security and emergency service continuity.
  • Prioritise accessible environments for staff, patients, visitors, detainees and the public.
  • Use digital infrastructure, low-carbon systems and safe circulation networks as core requirements rather than add-ons.
  • Keep capacity models realistic so the building performs during normal operation, peak demand and emergency conditions.

Category comparison

How the standards differ by facility type

Hospital

Health and care environments

  • Designed for patient safety, staff wellbeing and emergency response.
  • High infection-control standards and ventilation performance.
  • Flexible wards and diagnostic rooms to accommodate population change.
  • Capacity model: 500–1,500 people at peak operational demand.
Fire service

Emergency response readiness

  • Fast-turnout layouts, secure equipment staging and crew welfare areas.
  • Standby power and command continuity for severe weather or incidents.
  • Adaptable response spaces for multi-agency command and resilience drills.
  • Capacity model: 40–80 crew members and rapid mobilisation footprint.
Police

Public-facing safety and investigation

  • Safe public access, secure operational circulation and trauma-informed spaces.
  • Digital evidence handling, secure IT and resilient command systems.
  • Flexible community service spaces for neighbourhood engagement.
  • Capacity model: 150–350 users during peak daily operations.
Prison

Secure, safe and rehabilitative

  • Secure movement patterns, safe cell design and de-escalation spaces.
  • Healthcare, education and training spaces to support safer reintegration.
  • Operational and environmental resilience for long-term use.
  • Capacity model: 900–1,600 residents with support staffing.
School

Learning environments for growth

  • Classroom flexibility, accessible circulation, daylight-rich teaching spaces and safe outdoor learning.
  • Healthy air quality, climate resilience and efficient energy use for long operating hours.
  • Spaces for community use, safeguarding and active travel access.
  • Capacity model: 300–1,500 pupils, staff and visitors.
College

Skills and technical learning

  • Adaptive teaching and workshop spaces linked to local labour market needs.
  • Digital learning infrastructure, student support and public-facing professional facilities.
  • Strong connections between education, training and local employers.
  • Capacity model: 800–3,000 learners and staff.
University

Research, resilience and civic life

  • Research and teaching spaces, libraries, student facilities and accommodation for large campus populations.
  • High-capacity digital connectivity, resilient services and low-carbon energy systems.
  • Campus design supports both academic excellence and public benefit.
  • Capacity model: 5,000–25,000 students, staff and visitors.

Minimum assurance

Design assurance and operational checks

Safety

All facilities must prove safe internal movement, secure boundaries, clear emergency routes and reliable access controls.

Resilience

Back-up power, water continuity, communications resilience and flood or heat protections are mandatory.

Accessibility

Accessible design is not a separate add-on; it is part of the core brief for staff, patients, detainees and visitors.

Adaptability

Projects should be able to absorb demographic, demand and service-model change without major disruption.
