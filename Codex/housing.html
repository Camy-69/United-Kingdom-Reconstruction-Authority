Delivery ambition

The housing programme will deliver a larger, healthier and more resilient residential stock across the UK. The priority is not only to build more homes, but to make them easier to heat, easier to access, easier to maintain and better aligned with long-term local demand.

  • Expand the supply of affordable, energy-efficient and resilient housing in towns and cities across the UK.
  • Retrofit and upgrade existing stock to improve warmth, safety, accessibility and operating efficiency.
  • Improve the quality of neighbourhoods by linking housing with services, public realm and local jobs.
  • Target lower-cost delivery methods while protecting long-term performance and public value.

Future-proof homes by design

Every new home and retrofit is designed around a 60-year service life rather than a minimum-compliance standard. The objective is to create homes that remain warm, healthy, accessible and affordable to live in as climate conditions, household needs and technology requirements evolve.

  • Warm and healthy — high insulation, airtight construction, secure ventilation, flood resilience, acoustic performance and low internal air pollution levels.
  • Accessible and adaptable — step-free access, wider doors and circulation, future-proof bathrooms, adaptable rooms for ageing, working or caring at home.
  • Smart and connected — fibre-first connectivity, smart metering, EV charging, solar-ready roofs, battery-ready electrical capacity and digital monitoring.
  • Low-carbon and efficient — heat-pump readiness, low embodied carbon materials, durable components, passive design and lower whole-life operating costs.

Office, warehouse and other standards

  • Offices — flexible floorplates, high-quality daylight, acoustic separation, hybrid-working layouts, cycle parking, resilient digital infrastructure and low-energy cooling.
  • Warehouses and logistics — efficient clear-height layouts, safe pedestrian and vehicle separation, solar-ready roofs, EV charging, robust loading access and carbon monitoring for day-to-day operations.
  • Mixed-use neighbourhood schemes — homes, childcare, health, retail and local services designed around walkable streets, active travel and accessible public space.
  • Civic and public buildings — emergency resilience, inclusive access, service continuity and adaptable rooms that can be used for community, care or public administration functions.
  • Industrial and employment spaces — ventilation standards, fire safety, resilient power, flexible use to support new manufacturing and logistics models as industry changes.
